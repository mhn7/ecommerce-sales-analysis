# Key Findings

## 1. Overall Business Performance

### Key Metrics

- Total revenue: $5,109,775.74
- Total orders: 5,000
- Unique customers: 989
- Average order value: $1,021.96
- Total units sold: 20,224

### Initial Observation

The dataset contains 5,000 e-commerce orders generated by 989 unique customers, producing approximately $5.11 million in total revenue. The average order value is approximately $1,022, with 20,224 units sold across the dataset.

## 2. Product & Regional Performance

### Category Performance

- Electronics generated the highest revenue at $1,829,899.22 across 1,777 orders.
- Clothing ranked second with $1,531,931.72 in revenue across 1,531 orders.
- Home generated $982,083.92 from 969 orders.
- Beauty generated the lowest total revenue at $765,860.88, but had the highest average order value at $1,059.28.

### Regional Performance

- West generated the highest revenue at $1,345,582.16 across 1,289 orders.
- North generated $1,281,508.45 across 1,276 orders.
- South generated $1,246,640.90 across 1,208 orders.
- East generated $1,236,044.23 across 1,227 orders.

### Initial Observation

Electronics is the strongest revenue-generating category primarily because it combines the highest order volume with a relatively high average order value. Beauty has the highest average order value, but its lower order volume results in the lowest total revenue among the four categories.

Regional revenue is relatively balanced, although the West leads the four regions in both order volume and total revenue.



## 3. Revenue Trends

### Yearly Revenue Performance

- Revenue declined gradually from $371,824.30 in 2022 to $349,802.33 in 2025.
- Revenue recovered in 2026, increasing by 10.45% to $386,369.69.
- Revenue remained relatively volatile in subsequent years, with notable increases in 2030 (+7.97%), 2032 (+5.84%), and 2033 (+5.73%).
- 2033 recorded the highest full-year revenue at $408,725.72.
- Revenue declined by 11.13% in 2034 to $363,239.55.

### Important Data Limitation

The dataset ends on September 9, 2035. Therefore, 2035 represents only a partial year and should not be directly compared with complete calendar years. The apparent 34.28% year-over-year decline in 2035 should not be interpreted as a full-year business decline.

### Initial Observation

Revenue does not follow a consistent upward or downward trend. Instead, the business shows considerable year-to-year volatility, with periods of decline followed by recoveries. The strongest complete year in the dataset is 2033, while 2035 cannot be reliably evaluated as a full-year period because the data is incomplete.

## 4. Discount Behavior

### Discount Performance

| Discount Band | Orders | Average Quantity per Order | Average Order Value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0–10% | 1,260 | 4.05 | $1,175.15 |
| 10–20% | 1,413 | 4.02 | $1,052.65 |
| 20–30% | 1,476 | 4.11 | $958.78 |
| 30%+ | 851 | 3.98 | $853.74 |

### Key Observation

Higher discount bands are associated with progressively lower average order values. AOV falls from $1,175.15 for orders receiving 0–10% discounts to $853.74 for orders receiving discounts of 30% or more.

Order volume does not increase consistently with larger discounts. Orders peak at 1,476 in the 20–30% discount band before falling to 851 orders in the 30%+ band.

The correlation between discount percentage and revenue is -0.139, indicating a weak negative linear relationship.

### Interpretation

The data does not provide evidence that larger discounts generate more orders or higher revenue. Instead, heavily discounted orders tend to have lower order values, while the highest discount band also has substantially fewer orders.

Because this is observational data, these relationships should be interpreted as associations rather than proof that discounts directly cause lower revenue or order volume.

## 5. Customer Retention & Repeat Behavior

### Customer Type

| Customer Type | Customers | Total Revenue | Revenue Share | Revenue per Customer |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Repeat | 950 | $5,072,972.55 | 99.28% | $5,339.97 |
| One-time | 39 | $36,803.19 | 0.72% | $943.67 |

### Key Observation

Repeat customers account for 950 of the 989 customers and generate 99.28% of total revenue. One-time customers represent only 39 customers and contribute just 0.72% of revenue.

This indicates that repeat purchasing is the primary driver of revenue in the dataset.

### Time to Second Order

| Time to Second Order | Customers | Customer Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0–30 days | 25 | 2.63% |
| 31–90 days | 65 | 6.84% |
| 91–180 days | 79 | 8.32% |
| 181–365 days | 146 | 15.37% |
| 366–730 days | 209 | 22.00% |
| 730+ days | 426 | 44.84% |

### Interpretation

Although repeat customers generate the vast majority of revenue, repeat purchases often occur after relatively long intervals. Nearly 45% of repeat customers took more than two years to place their second order.

This suggests an opportunity to investigate customer re-engagement strategies, particularly for customers approaching or exceeding long periods without another purchase.

The analysis shows a strong association between repeat purchasing and revenue, but it does not establish why customers return or whether specific marketing actions caused them to return.

## 6. Customer Satisfaction & Delivery

### Customer Ratings

- The average customer rating is 2.97 out of 5.
- Ratings range from 1.0 to 5.0.

### Delivery Performance

- Average delivery time is approximately 6 days.
- The correlation between delivery time and customer rating is -0.018, indicating essentially no linear relationship in this dataset.

### Key Observation

Customer satisfaction is relatively mediocre, with an average rating of 2.97 out of 5.

However, delivery time does not show a meaningful linear relationship with customer ratings. Customers receiving faster deliveries did not consistently provide higher ratings than customers with longer delivery times.

### Interpretation

The analysis does not provide evidence that delivery time is a major linear driver of customer ratings in this dataset. Other factors, such as product quality, pricing, or customer experience, may contribute more strongly to satisfaction.

This result should be interpreted as an association rather than evidence that delivery speed has no effect on customer satisfaction.
